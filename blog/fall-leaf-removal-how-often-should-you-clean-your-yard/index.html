Fall Leaf Removal: How Often Should You Clean Your Yard?

Updated October 1, 2026  ·  Leaf Removal  ·  By Mike Harmon, Owner & Licensed Applicator

Every fall the same question comes up in Noblesville, Carmel, Westfield, and Fishers. Do you clear the leaves once at the end, or several times as they fall? The answer depends on how many trees you have and how long the leaves sit on the grass. Here's the timing for Hamilton County and what Purdue's turf research says about leaves on lawns. We also pulled together the 2026 leaf pickup rules for each city.

The short answer

  • Light leaves (you can still see the grass): mow them into the lawn. No removal needed.
  • One or two big trees: one cleanup in mid to late November.
  • Several mature trees: two or three visits from late October into early December.

Either way, don't let a layer you can't see through sit on the lawn for weeks or go into winter.

When Leaves Fall in Hamilton County

Peak fall color for most of Indiana usually lands in mid to late October. Leaf drop follows peak color and runs into November. Some trees hang on much longer. Purdue Extension notes that Callery pear (the Bradford pear planted in so many subdivisions) keeps dark crimson leaves late into the fall. Some oaks hold dead leaves well into winter, a habit called marcescence.

Frost matters too. National Weather Service normals put the average first freeze (32°F) at October 18 in Anderson and October 26 at the Indianapolis airport, with a hard freeze (28°F) by October 31 and November 5. Most of the leaf drop lands on grass that's still green and still needs light.

What Purdue Says About Leaves on Your Lawn

Purdue's turf program has studied this for years, and the answer isn't just "rake everything."

Mowing light leaves into the lawn is fine. Purdue's turf team calls mowing leaves into the turf the easiest way to dispose of them. Their research found mulched leaves did not increase thatch, disease, or weeds, and did not change soil pH. The Hamilton County Soil and Water Conservation District gives the same advice.

Heavy layers are a different story. Purdue's line is to rake when there are so many leaves you can't see the turf underneath. A Purdue turf professor once compared a heavy leaf layer to leaving a piece of plywood on the yard. Purdue also notes that heavily wooded lots may require leaf disposal because there are simply too many leaves to mulch.

Leaves left over winter invite snow mold. Purdue's plant diagnostic lab reports that lawns with heavy leaf debris through winter are more susceptible to snow mold, and the debris thins the turf by blocking light. You find out in March, when gray, matted circles show up as the snow melts. If that happens, see our lawn disease control service.

How Often to Clear Leaves: Three Schedules

Light leaf cover. If you can see grass through the leaves, keep mowing and let the mower shred them. That's what Purdue recommends, and it's free.

One or two large trees. One cleanup in mid to late November, after most of the drop and before the ground freezes, usually works. The early leaves sit for a few weeks, but a light to moderate layer does limited harm in that time.

Several mature trees. One big cleanup doesn't work here. Waiting for the last leaf means the first leaves have been on the grass for six weeks or more, packed down by rain. On these lots we schedule:

  • Late October: clear the early drop, mostly maples, before it mats down.
  • Mid November: clear the main drop. This is the heaviest volume.
  • Early December, if needed: catch the oaks and pears that held on.

City Leaf Pickup in 2026

If you handle leaves yourself, how you get rid of them depends on where you live. Here's what each city or its hauler has posted. Rules change, so check the linked page before you set anything out.

Noblesville. The city runs both bagged and loose leaf pickup from October 12 through December 11, 2026. Bagged leaves must go in the free compostable bags the city provides, available at fire stations, City Hall, the Parks Department, and the Street Department. Bags go between the street and sidewalk on trash day, 40 pounds max. For loose leaves, rake them to the edge of the street (not into it) and keep them separate from brush. The vacuum trucks run a continuous loop that takes about four weeks to cover the city, so a loose pile can sit a while. After December 11 the city asks residents to bag.

Carmel. Trash pickup is through Republic Services. From October 15 to December 15, each household can set out 20 extra bags or bundles of landscape waste per week. Carmel doesn't run a leaf vacuum program.

Westfield. Weekly yard waste pickup is a seasonal subscription through WM, not a free city service. If you drop yard waste at a city bulk trash day, Westfield asks for paper bags, not plastic.

Fishers. Leaf and yard waste pickup is part of the city's Republic Services contract. See the City of Fishers waste page for the current bag rules and extra-bag months.

Geist (Lawrence side). The City of Lawrence collects compost weekly in April and November only, up to 15 paper bags or marked containers per address. Homes on the Fishers side of Geist follow the Fishers rules.

Burning isn't an option in most of the county. Noblesville, Carmel, and Westfield all ban burning leaves inside city limits, and Carmel's fine runs up to $500 per violation. More on that in our post on burning leaf piles.

If we do your leaf removal, you can skip all of this. We haul everything off the property the same day, so you don't bag anything or wait on a vacuum truck.

Two Fall Mistakes That Undo the Work

Scalping the last mow. A lot of people drop the mower deck way down for the final cut. Don't go below 3 inches. Purdue recommends mowing at 3 inches or higher until the grass goes dormant. We mow at 3.75 to 4 inches all season and take the last cut down to about 3.

Fertilizing under a leaf layer. Purdue recommends a final nitrogen application within a week or two of the last mowing, usually early to mid November, while the grass is still green. That fertilizer needs to reach the grass, which means the leaves need to be off first. More in when to apply winterizer fertilizer.

Leaf Removal FAQ

Is it better to mulch leaves or remove them?
Mulch them if you can still see grass through the layer. Purdue research found mulched leaves cause no harm to the lawn. Remove them when the layer is thick enough to hide the grass, or when there are more leaves than your mower can shred in one pass.

When does Noblesville pick up leaves in 2026?
October 12 through December 11, 2026, for both bagged leaves (city compostable bags only) and loose leaves raked to the street edge. After December 11 the city asks residents to bag.

Can I burn leaves in Noblesville, Carmel, or Westfield?
No. All three cities ban leaf burning inside city limits. Carmel's fine is up to $500 per violation.

Can leaves really kill grass?
Yes. A heavy layer blocks light and holds moisture against the grass, and Purdue reports that lawns with heavy leaf debris through winter are more prone to snow mold.

How much does leaf removal cost in Hamilton County?
Our leaf removal typically runs $150 to $500 per visit depending on lot size and leaf volume. Heavily wooded lots cost more because of the sheer volume.
