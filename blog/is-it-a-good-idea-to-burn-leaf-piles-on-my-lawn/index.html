Can You Burn Leaves in Hamilton County? City Rules and Better Options

Updated October 16, 2026  ·  Leaf Removal  ·  By Mike Harmon, Owner & Licensed Applicator

In most of Hamilton County, you can't burn leaves. Noblesville, Carmel, and Westfield all ban it inside city limits, and Fishers only allows burning wood. Even where state law allows it, the fire has to be in a closed burn barrel, not a pile on the lawn. Here's what the rules say in each city and what to do with the leaves instead.

What Indiana State Law Allows

The Indiana Department of Environmental Management's open burning rules do allow residential burning of leaves, brush, and clean wood in some places, but only under strict conditions:

  • It has to be in a noncombustible container with enclosed sides, like a burn barrel. A pile on the ground doesn't qualify.
  • The fire has to be out before sunset, and someone has to stay with it the whole time.
  • No burning in high wind, in stagnant air, or on Air Quality Action Days.
  • It's not allowed at all where a city or county ordinance bans it.

That last condition is the one that matters around here. Most Hamilton County cities have their own bans.

The Rules in Noblesville, Carmel, Westfield, and Fishers

Noblesville: The city's open burning ordinance prohibits burning yard waste and leaves anywhere in city limits. Recreational and cooking fires need a free permit from the fire department.

Carmel: The Carmel Fire Department's open burning guidelines prohibit open burning in the city. The exceptions are gas and propane grills and small recreational campfires using untreated wood.

Westfield: The city's fire prevention ordinance says leaf burning is strictly prohibited within city limits.

Fishers: The Fishers Fire Department's permitted burning rules allow burning only between 9 a.m. and 7 p.m., at least 50 feet from any structure, with an adult and a hose or extinguisher on hand. Only wood products can be burned. If you're not sure whether something is allowed, call the fire department at (317) 595-3200 before you light it.

Rules can also change for a season. Local fire chiefs and councils issue burn bans during dry weather, and the state posts them on its burn ban page. A dry, windy October afternoon is when leaves are most tempting to burn, and it's also when burn bans go into effect.

Why Burning Leaves Is a Bad Idea Anyway

The smoke. The EPA's guidance on backyard fires says never to burn yard waste because it creates more smoke and can be toxic. It notes that children, older adults, and people with heart or lung disease are the most sensitive to it. In a neighborhood with houses 20 feet apart, that smoke goes straight into your neighbors' windows.

The lawn. A burning pile puts a lot of heat on one spot. The grass under it dies, and you're left with a bare patch to reseed in the spring.

The fire risk. Dry leaves burn fast, and embers travel. A pile that gets away from you can reach a fence, a shed, or the house.

What to Do With the Leaves Instead

Mulch them with the mower. Purdue's turf team says mulching leaves into the lawn won't increase thatch or disease and has no effect on soil pH. This works for a light to moderate layer. If the leaves are deep enough to hide the grass, mulching won't keep up.

Use your city's leaf collection. Each city handles it differently:

  • Noblesville runs leaf vacuum trucks from October 12 to December 11 this year, but a full loop can take up to four weeks to reach your street.
  • Carmel takes up to 20 extra bags a week during the fall leaf season.
  • Fishers takes up to 20 extra bags a week, but only in April and November.
  • Westfield doesn't offer free leaf pickup. Fall leaf collection is a paid subscription through WM.

Compost them. If you have room, leaves make good compost mixed with grass clippings. It takes several months, and you end up with free material for your garden beds.

Have them hauled away. For properties with mature trees, the leaf volume is often more than a mower or the city can keep up with. Our leaf removal clears the lawn, the beds, the fence lines, and the driveway, and everything leaves with us the same day.

Leaf Burning FAQ

Can I burn leaves in Noblesville?
No. Noblesville's open burning ordinance prohibits burning yard waste and leaves within city limits. Recreational and cooking fires need a free permit from the fire department.

Can I burn leaves in Carmel or Westfield?
No. Carmel prohibits open burning except grills and small recreational fires, and Westfield's fire prevention ordinance says leaf burning is strictly prohibited in city limits.

Can I burn leaves in Fishers?
Fishers only allows burning wood products, between 9 a.m. and 7 p.m. and at least 50 feet from structures. Call the Fishers Fire Department at (317) 595-3200 before burning anything else.

Is burning leaves legal anywhere in Indiana?
In some places, yes. State rules allow residential burning of leaves only in a noncombustible container with enclosed sides, attended, out before sunset, and only where no local ordinance or burn ban prohibits it.

What's the best way to get rid of leaves?
For a light layer, mulch them into the lawn with your mower. Purdue says this won't increase thatch or disease. For heavy leaf cover, use your city's collection program or have them removed and hauled away.
