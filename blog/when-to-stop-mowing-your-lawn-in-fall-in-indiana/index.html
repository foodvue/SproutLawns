When to Stop Mowing Your Lawn in the Fall in Indiana

Updated October 16, 2026  ·  Lawn Mowing  ·  By Mike Harmon, Owner & Licensed Applicator

Keep mowing until your grass stops growing. In Hamilton County that's usually sometime in November. For the last mow, drop the height to about 3 inches. Purdue University recommends mowing right up until the lawn goes dormant, and a slightly shorter final cut helps prevent snow mold over the winter. Here's how to tell when you're done and how to set the lawn up for spring.

Keep Mowing Until the Grass Goes Dormant

Purdue Extension's advice on fall mowing and leaf removal is to keep mowing at the recommended height until the lawn goes dormant in late fall. There's no set date. Cool-season grass keeps growing through the cool weather of October and slows down as the weather turns cold.

In our area, the National Weather Service puts the average hard freeze (28°F) at October 31 in Anderson and November 5 at the Indianapolis airport. After a warm fall, Hamilton County lawns can still need a cut in mid-November. After a cold one, growth can stop by the end of October.

The practical rule: if you go 10 to 14 days and the lawn doesn't look any taller, you're done for the season.

Cut the Last Mow to About 3 Inches

During the season we mow at 3.75 to 4 inches. For the last mow, lower the deck to about 3 inches.

The reason is snow mold. Purdue's turfgrass disease guide says pink snow mold is most common on Kentucky bluegrass and ryegrass lawns kept at 3 inches or taller. It recommends keeping fall turf at 2½ to 3 inches so the grass doesn't fold over and mat down under the snow. That matted layer holds moisture against the soil, which is where the disease starts. You see it in March as circular, matted patches when the snow melts.

Don't go much lower than that. Scalping the lawn before winter exposes the base of the plant to cold and drying winds. Shorter than summer height is the goal, not a buzz cut.

Get the Leaves Off First

The last mows of the year overlap with peak leaf drop. Purdue says mulching leaves with a rotary mower several times through the fall is a good way to handle them. A light layer chops up and breaks down into the lawn.

A heavy layer is different. Purdue warns that heavy leaf debris left on the lawn through winter thins the turf and makes it more prone to snow mold. Mowing over a thick layer doesn't remove it. It just turns it into a mat. If you can't see grass through the leaves, get them off the lawn before the final cut. Don't Mow Frozen or Frosty Grass

Purdue says to stay off frost-covered or frozen turf, which means no mowing and no leaf removal. Frozen grass blades break when you walk or drive on them, and the damage shows up as brown tracks in the spring. On frosty November mornings, wait until the frost melts.

After the Last Mow

  • Winterizer. If it isn't down yet, apply it around the last mow while the grass is still green. Our winterizer guide covers Purdue's timing.
  • Clear the lawn. Get leaves, toys, tarps, and equipment off the grass. Anything left sitting on the lawn all winter kills the turf underneath.
  • Clean up the mower. Sharpen the blade and service the engine now so it's ready for the first mow in April.

Fall Mowing FAQ

When should I stop mowing my lawn in Indiana?
When the grass stops growing, usually sometime in November in central Indiana. Purdue recommends mowing at the normal height until the lawn goes dormant. If the lawn doesn't look taller after 10 to 14 days, you're done for the season.

How short should I cut my grass for the last mow?
About 3 inches, down from the 3.75 to 4 inches we use during the season. Purdue recommends keeping fall turf at 2½ to 3 inches to prevent the matting that leads to snow mold. Don't scalp it.

Can I mow over leaves instead of raking them?
Yes, if the layer is light. Purdue says mulching leaves with a rotary mower several times through the fall works well. If the leaves are thick enough to hide the grass, remove them first.

Is it bad to mow frosty grass?
Yes. Purdue says to avoid mowing and other lawn work on frost-covered or frozen turf. Wait until the frost melts.
